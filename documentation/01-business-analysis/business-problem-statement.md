# M03-01 — Business Problem Statement
Business Problem

Local shops and small grocery stores in South African townships play an important role in the local retail economy, but many operate under significant challenges. These include limited capital, weak digital literacy, a lack of digital inventory management systems, and reliance on intuition when making stock and restocking decisions.

Shop owners may overstock products that have low demand or short expiry periods. This can result in expired stock, food waste, and money being tied up in products that are not selling. At the same time, high demand products may be understocked, resulting in stockouts, lost sales, and customers being unable to find the products they need.

The problem is made worse by the lack of historical sales analysis and predictive tools. Without reliable data analysis, stock decisions are often reactive rather than based on historical sales patterns and expected demand.

3. Business Impact

The combination of overstocking and stockouts can reduce the profitability and sustainability of small retail businesses.

Overstocking can result in:

Expired products.
Increased food waste.
Capital being tied up in unsold stock.
Limited space for products with higher demand.

Understocking can result in:

Stockouts of high demand products.
Lost sales.
Customer dissatisfaction.
Reduced product availability.

These problems are particularly important for small shops because they often operate with limited financial resources and have less capacity to absorb losses caused by poor inventory decisions.

4. Need for a Solution

RetailAI aims to address the inventory management problem by using historical sales and inventory data to support better stock decisions.

The proposed system will use Python based machine learning and data analysis to help identify demand patterns and provide information that can support forecasting and restocking decisions.

The system is intended to help small retail businesses move from mainly reactive stock decisions towards more data informed inventory management.

5. Community and Economic Relevance

Improving inventory management in local township shops can provide benefits beyond the individual business.

A solution that helps reduce unnecessary stock waste and improve product availability can support:

Reduced food waste.
Better availability of products for local residents.
Improved sustainability of small retail businesses.
Support for local employment.
Greater resilience within underserved communities.
Strengthening of the informal and small retail sector.
6. Proposed Business Response

RetailAI will investigate the use of artificial intelligence and machine learning to analyse retail sales and inventory data.

The system is intended to provide information that can support:

Demand forecasting.
Identification of high and low demand products.
Identification of potential stockout risks.
More informed restocking decisions.
Identification of slow moving or potentially expiring stock.

The proposed approach is designed to be achievable using Python based machine learning and is aligned with the project's focus on applying AI to a real retail business problem.

7. Problem Statement

Small shops and grocery stores in South African townships face difficulties managing inventory because of limited resources, limited digital inventory systems, and reliance on intuition rather than historical data when making stock decisions. This contributes to both overstocking and stockouts, which can lead to expired stock, food waste, tied up capital, lost sales, and customer dissatisfaction.

RetailAI addresses this business problem by investigating a Python based AI system that analyses sales and inventory data to support demand forecasting, stock monitoring, and more informed restocking decisions.
